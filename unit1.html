Unit 1: Lens-Based Analysis Essay

During my first few weeks at college, I felt like I was supposed to already be comfortable. Everyone around me seemed to have friends, plans, and something exciting happening. I was meeting people and having fun too, but college still did not feel like home. One night, I came back to my dorm and started missing my family. I missed small things, like talking to them without calling or being around people who already knew me. What bothered me was feeling like I should have moved past that already.

At first, I kept those feelings to myself. When people asked how college was going, it felt easier to talk about new friends or things I had done. I did not want to seem unhappy with my decision to come here, because I wasn’t. Eventually, I mentioned feeling homesick to a few friends, and they admitted they felt similarly. That surprised me because I thought they had already adjusted. I assumed that because they seemed comfortable around me, they felt that way alone too.

That conversation made me notice how much more easily we shared exciting experiences than uncomfortable ones. I had taken those conversations as proof that everyone was adjusting faster than me. However, by only sharing positive things myself, I might have been giving others that same impression. Maybe the pressure to adjust comes partly from what we leave out of conversations.

I cannot assume everyone who seems happy is hiding homesickness. Some people might adjust sooner, while others prefer keeping their feelings private. Still, how many students feel behind because they compare their private feelings to what others share? If nobody mentions struggling, it becomes easy to believe you are the only one.

I think part of the problem is feeling like we have to seem adjusted before we actually are. Missing home and enjoying college can happen together, but at first, I didn’t think I could feel both ways. Opening up did not make my homesickness disappear, but it made me feel less alone. Feeling understood helped me feel more comfortable than pretending I already was.
